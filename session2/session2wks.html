    1.

    Suppose you want to find the volume of a solid, \(S\text{,}\) where the base of the solid is the region bounded by \(f(x) = x^2\) and \(g(x) = 4\) and cross sections of \(S\) are squares that are perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis (see figures below).
    🔗

    (a)

    Should this problem be set up in terms of the variable \(x\) or \(y\text{?}\) Why?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    The formula for the area of a square is \(bh\text{,}\) where \(b\) is the base and \(h\) is the height. Find mathematical expressions for \(b\) and \(h\text{.}\)

    🔗

    (c)

    To find the volume of \(S\) we need to sum up the areas of all the square cross sections, which means we need to set up a definite integral. Find the limits of integration of the definite integral that would find the volume of \(S\text{.}\)

    Write the integral that would find the volume of \(S\text{.}\)
